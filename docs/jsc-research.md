# JSC Methodology Research

Research compiled 2026-04-04 for calibrating the AI council tone and behavior.

## Key Finding

Real JSCs are **warm peer support groups with structural accountability** — NOT interrogations, boot camps, or tough-love coaching. The core emotional transformation is converting "fear, insecurity, and anxiety into hope, motivation, accountability, and confidence."

The challenge comes from the **process** (persistent commitments, structured agendas, peer visibility), not from confrontational personalities.

## Key Concepts from Research

### Tone
- **Warmth is the dominant note, accountability is the undertone** — the warmth creates safety that makes accountability possible
- Members are more open with their JSC than with family or close friends
- Celebrations of small wins are built into every meeting
- The "Orangutan Room" concept: simply articulating your problem to sympathetic listeners creates clarity

### Real Meeting Structure (from Cassie's doc + Phyl's overview)

Every session follows the same **5-part skeleton**:

1. **Check-Ins** (~10-15 min) — light personal prompt + emotional pulse (1-10 scale) + professional updates + "do you need help?"
2. **Main Exercise** (~30-60 min) — varies by session number (see curriculum below)
3. **Requests for Help (RFH)** (~10-25 min) — members bring specific asks
4. **Homework & Logistics** (~5 min)
5. **Feedback & Check-Outs** (~5 min) — Plus/Delta + closing prompt

### 10-Session Curriculum

| Session | Theme | Exercise |
|---------|-------|----------|
| 0 | Trust-building | Open introductions, emotional landscape, no homework |
| 1 | Charter & Introductions | Review charter, extended intros, daily routines |
| 2 | Mnookin Two-Pager Step 1 | Must-Nots & Must-Haves paired exercises |
| 3 | Mnookin Two-Pager Step 2 | Each member presents their Two-Pager draft |
| 4 | Gratitude House | Share results of Gratitude House exercise |
| 5 | Listening Tour | Report on first 3+ conversations |
| 6 | Listening Tour (continued) | Continue sharing insights from conversations |
| 7 | Candidate-Market Fit | Present 1-sentence job search strategy |
| 8 | LinkedIn/Resume Rehab | Paired LinkedIn/resume review exercises |
| 9+ | Networking | Megibow Dashboard, target companies, networking groups |
| 10+ | Interview Prep | Paired interview exercises, Job Mission with OKRs |
| 11+ | Negotiation | Salary exercise, four legs of negotiation stool |

### Moderator Role
- **A peer, not an authority** — first among equals
- Uses provided agenda templates rather than improvising
- More of a "meeting chair" than a coach
- Personality: organized, warm, committed to showing up
- Key moderator practices: listen (3 audiences: speaker, group, self), keep time, enforce charter, model vulnerability

### How Challenge Works
- Challenge is **structural, not interpersonal** — commitments persist and are reviewed, creating natural accountability
- Members offer perspective: "here's what I see from where I sit" and "have you considered..."
- It is NOT: "your resume is garbage" or "you're making excuses" or "let me tell you what's wrong"
- The Candidate-Market Fit framework is itself a form of gentle challenge
- "Croesus' Curse" — when members twist feedback to fit existing bias. Moderator should expect this, not fight it early. Trust takes time.

### Group Dynamics
- Collaborative and peer-based: "Instead of competing or comparing, members support one another"
- Groups intentionally composed of similar career stages but different industries (reduces competition)
- Interaction extends beyond meetings into ongoing Slack/message support

### Key Exercises & Tools (from the book)
- **Mnookin Two-Pager** — living doc: what you love/hate, must-haves, must-nots, career goals
- **Gratitude House** — reflect on who helped your career, write thank-you notes, builds Listening Tour contact list
- **Listening Tour** — 3-5 Reverse Exit Interviews + Golden Question conversations + recruiter conversations
- **Candidate-Market Fit (CMF)** — 1-sentence job search strategy aligning aspirations with market reality
- **Megibow Dashboard** — tracking tool for networking activity (outreach, meetings, calls)
- **Jeff's Opportunity Screener** — evaluate job prospects against CMF criteria
- **Job Mission with OKRs** — your version of the job description, used for negotiation
- **Rose/Thorn/Bud** — check-in framework (what's going well / challenge / emerging)
- **I Like, I Wish, I Wonder** — feedback framework (from Stanford d.school)

## Implications for Our App

1. ~~**Prizrak as moderator is wrong**~~ — DONE: Replaced with Jordan (warm facilitator)
2. ~~**Prompt tone needs recalibration**~~ — DONE: Rewritten to be warm and curious
3. **The 7-phase structure was wrong** — The real JSC uses a 5-part skeleton (check-in, exercise, RFH, homework, checkout) repeated each session, with exercises progressing across the 10-session curriculum. Need to rewrite phases.
4. **Sessions need to be number-aware** — Session 0 is trust-building, session 2 is must-haves/must-nots, session 7 is CMF, etc. The AI should guide the user through the right exercise for their session number.
5. **Emotional pulse (1-10) should be in every session** — this is central to the real JSC
6. **Personal check-in prompts build trust** — every session starts with something human ("What did you want to be when you grew up?"), not business
7. **Exercises need AI adaptation** — paired exercises become AI-guided solo exercises (AI plays the partner role in must-haves/must-nots interviews, Mnookin feedback, LinkedIn rehab, etc.)
